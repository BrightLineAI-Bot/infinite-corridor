# Changelog

## Unreleased

- Release 257 — the Lightning Giant, a new roaming world boss (three tries, the level-8 strength floor, and a canyon home). He's a big armoured robot in the Armored Core mould, about as tall as the Astral Worm is long, built of lit, overlapping plates with blue trim, with a plume of four Tesla-coil jets fanned over the back of his head (the biggest leading), each firing a bolt-shaped burst of blue energy. He has lost one arm; its torn shoulder socket is his booster, erupting with energy as he leans into each dash. He swings a copper-wound Tesla mace in his remaining hand, and walks with a heavy, planted stride (the feet never slide):
  - **Arcs:** a sheaf of lightning links him to you and burns while you stay within reach.
  - **Shock:** a crackling mark you can step off.
  - **Stun counter:** every third lightning hit knocks you flat for half a second, shaking on the ground before you get back up, and the count shows as three pips under the HUD. Club blows count only while he's electrified.
  - **Menacing slam:** he raises the club and stalks you. When you come within reach he slams it down (or fires a short bolt from his chest), up to twice. Keep away and he fires two quick chest beams after you, one after the other, the second turning a little toward you, before he lowers it.
  - **Charging upswing:** he burst-dashes in and swings up. Sometimes he dashes twice without swinging, or goes dash-swing-dash-swing. After his last swing he sometimes leaps, rains bolts or dashes at you in a crackle of arcs, then lands, or comes down in a slam onto where you stand.
  - **Club swings:** a forward swing, then a re-aimed back-swing.
  - **Slam:** may call a delayed lightning blast from the sky where you stood, and may throw three bolts in a tight cone straight at you.
  - **Electrify:** he raises the club, the sky strikes it, and wrapped in a crackling haze he moves faster while bolts drop around him.
  - **Retreat:** he walks to a chasm, where a storm cloud gathers under him and carries him up into the sky.
  - **At sea:** you can meet him on open water, riding his big storm cloud instead of a boat, the back of it trailing off behind him in smaller and smaller billows and swinging round when he turns.
  - His Echo, Giant's Arc, sends lightning from your kills into up to three nearby foes.
- Release 257 — a Void Walker Supernova set off in an open-sea section, or one that is at least 70% ocean, boils the sea into fog: the whole section fogs over for 30 seconds, then clears (another Supernova there renews it).
- Release 253 review fix — a Walker who climbed down from or was knocked off the Astral Worm now gets his random chance to leave like any Walker fighting on foot (the seated check no longer mistakes "dismounted" for "mounted").
- Release 253 — the Void Walker now boards the Astral Worm visibly. He hurls his silver saddle disk at the Worm (it homes on a moving Worm, trailing violet), the disk latches on and its violet pillar rises, then he phases out in a column of violet light and phases in standing on the plinth. The disk does no damage, and he can still be hit on the ground until he phases in. If the Worm dies or retreats mid-throw, the boarding is cancelled where he stands.
- Release 253 — new rules for the Astral Worm and Void Walker pair:
  - **Hostile rider:** once on, he stays on through every form and through the player's death. Every 12 seconds he randomly stays on, leaps down to fight on foot, or leaves the fight. If the Worm retreats while he rides, he rides it home and vanishes with it.
  - **Hostile Walker on foot:** he keeps fighting even after the Worm dies or retreats, until he is killed, the player's tries run out, or he decides to leave.
  - **Death count:** the three tries count from the first monster met; a joining Walker shares the tries already spent.
  - **Helping Walker:** he never rides. The Worm mostly hunts the player but turns on him from time to time, and its attacks hurt whichever of you they catch. His attacks also hit the player where they pass, the player's attacks hit him, and he leaves once he has lost half his health.

- Release 251 saddle — the Void Walker now rides on a compact futuristic plinth: a gunmetal clamp grips the beast with violet light bleeding beneath it, and a silver disc platform with a glowing seam carries him. A violet light field rises from the platform to about his hips, painted both behind him and as a faint veil in front.

- Release 251 review fixes — the Gate Revenant has poise: rapid blows still interrupt it, but after 0.6 s of accumulated stun it shrugs off hit-stun for 2.4 s, so fast weapons can no longer lock it out of attacking. A retreating Void Walker may leave a land section by any open tile on its exit edge, not only the single gate tile. An overworld Supernova near a section edge now also scars the overlapping part of each neighbouring section, recorded for when you go there. The Astral Griffin's front legs are rebuilt as feathered raptor legs with scaled shanks, jointed toes and hooked talons, and the Walker's riding saddle is now a traditional tan leather saddle with a pad, stitched fender and steel stirrup. All four Griffin legs are reworked: lion hind legs with a muscled thigh, a backward hock and padded paws, and raptor forelegs with layered plume hems. The tail ends in a hair tuft, and the throat feathers are soft scallops. The flight-form beam now leaves from behind the mandibles, with expanding rings, a hot splash and sparks where it strikes the ground (a steady ring under reduced motion).

- Release 251 ocean extension: the Void Walker sails a small personal skiff and the Crystal throws lifted water; ocean Supernova retains combat but never converts or scars ocean water.

- Release 251 — the player-sized Void Walker roams ordinary dungeons and the overworld. Warned violet rends release hands, claws, swords, slashes, spikes and short-lived wraiths; rifts enable evasion and mixed tracking constellations; event horizons pull and pulse. Its Supernova consumes walls in the loaded circular region without revealing fog or completing objectives, then leaves permanent harmless smooth white ground with subtle red cracks. Saved scars persist in both worlds. Straight teleport-dash barrages leave one-second damaging afterimages. Retreat follows the nearest reachable physical dungeon doorway using normal terrain and one-level jumps, then disappears at the door; no fallback teleport.
- Release 251 — the Morphing Blob regenerates current HP while naturally growing, up to half its unchanged maximum HP over the existing two-minute growth. Its maximum health, growth geometry and separate bloblet-merge healing do not increase. Growth-cap, pause and reload handling prevent extra healing.

- Release 249 review fixes — pausing preserves the remaining Crystal grab time rather than silently breaking its hold. Mirror walls reflect at swept projectile contact, so fast arrows and bolts cannot slip through between frames.

- Release 249 continuation — the Psychic Crystal now patrols dungeon halls instead of circling its spawn. It floats over terrain and phases through dungeon walls, patrolling even off-screen. Its model rises smoothly over higher ground and settles down beyond it. It engages when it sees you and never starts a new attack through a wall. Its separate retreat still ends in the dungeon's water.
- Release 249 continuation — orbital lasers belong to the Psychic Crystal, not the Nanobot Knight. One to four sky beams follow you for four seconds, tracing spiral, square, circle, zigzag or slash patterns. Their scorch marks are harmless and fade in 1.5 seconds. The Knight gathers three seeker orbs, or six at half health and below; they float first, home for two seconds after launch, then keep flying straight. Interrupted gathers no longer leave immortal orbiting orbs. Existing saved beasts receive the corrected attack assignment without changing their health or hunt state. Recoil and hit-stun decay correctly again.
- Release 249 — the fifth world beast: the Psychic Crystal. It is a long faceted crystal floating at head height, with lesser shards circling it. Its aura tells you what it means to do: a hazy white at rest, red and larger when it attacks, violet just before it grabs. Its attacks:
  - a force push that throws you back;
  - a force pull that drags you in and lashes out as you arrive;
  - a command grab: dodge as it reaches, or once held (you cannot move) struggle free by dodging again and again, or it crushes you;
  - stones it lifts and flings at you one by one;
  - a mirror wall of light that sends your arrows and bolts back at you, while a blade passes straight through it.
  It hunts the overworld like the others and retreats to open water. It also haunts dungeons (from level 8, in ordinary dungeons well away from the Refuge), but only ones with water in them. There it waits near the water and, out of patience, sinks into it. It never counts as one of the dungeon's guardians, so it never stands between you and a clear. Also:
  - the Nanobot Knight no longer stays drawn scattered after the first plain-steel hit;
  - a weapon carrying an elemental glyph (Ignite, Chain, Volatile) now reaches the Knight as the Echo glyph does;
  - world beasts keep their own strength in dungeons.
- Release 247 — the Nanobot Knight's cape is no longer cloth: it is the swarm itself. A stream of dark motes (a few glowing red) pours off its shoulders and trails out behind it like a billowing plume of smoke, thinning to nothing at the tail. Its swordplay grows:
  - a lunging thrust with the sword outstretched, at you or cutting across along any of the eight ways, sometimes ending in a beam of energy off the tip that reaches about as far as a Cinder Pike's thrust;
  - an onslaught of up to six cuts: two cuts (forward, then back), and it may dash after you for two more, and again for two more. How far it takes it varies, and it goes longer when hurt;
  - a retreating slash, leaping back away from you while it cuts, which it sometimes uses instead of turning to mist when you attack it.
  Some of its cuts throw an arc of energy off the sword, reaching about a Verge Cleaver's swing farther, so it can strike from where you strike it.
- Release 245 — the fourth world beast: the Nanobot Knight. It is black plate with a red seam of light and nothing inside but a swarm, with a tall helm and red visor, a cape that billows behind it, a faint miasma haze hanging about it, and a long black greatsword with a red edge. Plain steel scatters it: an ordinary sword swing or a physical shot does no damage and it reforms. Spells, magic shots, glyph powers (burning, chaining, bursts), echoes and any weapon carrying the Echo glyph hurt it. Its attacks:
  - it slides in low with three sliding cuts in a row;
  - its arm opens into a cannon whose red beam tracks you while it charges, then fires straight;
  - swords fall out of the sky onto marked ground around and on you;
  - a lance of red light from the sky follows you, scorching the ground it passes;
  - seeker orbs gather and circle it, then go for you one at a time or all at once, homing for a moment and then flying straight;
  - a barrage of homing missiles bursts on whatever they meet, or by themselves when their fuel runs out;
  - it dissolves into mist and comes together beside you mid-cut.
  Whenever an attack comes at it (a blade, a shot, or a spell, magic or not), there is a random chance it simply isn't there: it turns to mist, even in the middle of its own attack, and reforms a few steps aside. When it gives up it streams home to the glass as a swarm. Also: an enemy's pose (shielded, spinning, tucked) no longer survives a reload, and the Mountain Turtle no longer retreats with its legs drawn in.
- Release 243 — the Mountain Turtle's second spin, the Icicle Cyclone. Drawn into its shell inside the ice tornado, it careens around the area, rebounding off walls and flinging volleys of icicles in every direction as it turns. It strikes whoever it hits. The first spin, which flings small twisters that wander and linger, stays; the Turtle uses both.
- Release 241 — the third world beast: the Mountain Turtle, a walking mausoleum about twice the Questing Beast's size. It is an ancient, weathered tortoise carrying a stepped stone ziggurat for a shell: cracked and chipped terraces with lichen, ice crystals grown on the ledges, a tomb door, and a broken, snow-capped crag for a summit. It has a long, plated neck and a heavy, half-shut, impassive eye. It is drawn flat-shaded and angular, so it stays cheap to render. It walks slowly and stamps a ring of heaved ground (jump it). It bursts forward in a straight rush that leaves fire, or ice that stings and slows and then bursts a couple of seconds later. It spins in its shell inside a tornado of ice, flinging off small twisters that wander, sting and slow you for ten seconds. It breathes a comet of ice that bursts where it lands and freezes the ground. Now and then it grows a shield of ice that nothing gets through, so wait it out. It dodges by pulling into its shell. Out of patience, it walks home to a canyon straight through whatever is in the way. Also: a fleeing beast now says it is fleeing when you follow it into the next section; it passes through settlements and the Questing Beast's ground instead of vanishing there; starting a retreat no longer stalls the game while it looks for home; and a beast that claims a section with nowhere to stand (all water) lets it go.
- Release 239 — world beasts go home. A beast that has neither killed you nor been killed after three minutes of fighting gives up and retreats to its home ground: the Morphing Blob to the nearest Blight Mire (or Sunken Fen), where it slides into the toxic pools; the Tremor Worm to the nearest Dune Sea, where it sinks into the sand. In retreat it cannot be hurt and finds its own way across the land, section after section: it goes around walls, needs a bridge to cross water, and bounds over canyons and chasms. The Worm swims under walls (though not under water), its back rolling through the ground and leaving a wake of churned earth that settles after a moment. (The Mountain Turtle, when it comes, will be too big to care and will go straight through.) Its body is its own business; only the ground under it counts. Follow it through each gate and watch it go; stop following and it is gone. Either way the hunt ends without costing you a try, and a new hunt can start elsewhere later. With no home within eight sections it flees six sections away and fades into the distance.
- Release 237 — the second world beast: the Tremor Worm. It hunts like the Blob (a rare claim on a wild section, tuned to your power, three tries, a bounty). It fights with its head out of a hole in the ground, then sinks and swims unseen under the earth to come up somewhere near you, biting whoever stands where it breaks through. Tentacles lash out of its maw along four lanes; it inhales and drags you toward the maw; it raises tentacles out of the ground around you, some spitting bursts of fireballs and some holding a burning laser that turns slowly after you, and they stay (up to six, three of them lasers) until you cut them down or it dies. You cannot outrun them: a tentacle left far behind sinks back into the ground after a moment and comes up again around you a second or two later, wounds and all; it dives and bursts up under you where the ground cracks; it lobs rocks that burst where they land; and it spews lava from its maw that spills across the ground like poured water, burns while it is hot and crusts over in a couple of seconds. Below half health it also slams out a shockwave. The Morphing Blob's ooze and bloblets now really keep off high ground: a raised plateau is drawn over the low tiles just north of it, and ooze landing there looked as if it lay on top, so nothing of the Blob's lands under a plateau's overhang either, bloblets turn back at its foot, and ground puddles are drawn at the height of the ground they lie on.
- Release 235 — the first new world beast: the Morphing Blob; and a slightly smaller Questing Beast. Like the Questing Beast, the Blob now and then claims a wild section (marked as danger on the Atlas), is tuned to your power, dodges, and gives you three tries before it moves on; killing it pays a bounty. It keeps growing while it lives until it fills its ground, burns whatever touches it, and spatters ooze when struck that lies on the ground, slowing and stinging, until it dies. It whips three tendrils at once, blows a jet of poison gas that pushes you back and poisons you, breathes poison clouds that roll away in every direction, throws off bloblets that wander and then crawl home to merge back in and heal it, and surges forward to engulf you. High ground (elevation 4 and up) stays above it: no ooze lands there, and neither its ooze nor its touch nor its surge can reach you while you stand on it. Every bloblet that makes it home also makes the Blob noticeably bigger. The Questing Beast is drawn about 15% smaller.
- Release 233 — the Tyrant Mosasaur's hunt actually works. In 231 it could never surface in a real ocean (the spot it rises from skipped every ocean tile, because ocean is marked as unwalkable ground). Now it rises from any ocean tile in range; a chance only counts once it has really surfaced; and coming back to a section, after a dungeon or a death too, always starts a fresh visit.
- Release 231 — the Tyrant Mosasaur hunts. Out on ocean water the sea is never quite safe: every 40 seconds you spend on it there is a 30% chance the Tyrant Mosasaur rises from the deep a few tiles away and comes for you (its shadow first, then the lock and the lunge). It can come up to twice in one visit to a section, even after you have killed it; leave and come back and the chances start again. Each kill gives its experience, and the first gives its leviathan trophy. It gives up if you leave the section.
- Release 229 — the Tyrant Mosasaur looks like a mosasaur. It has its own semi-segmented body now: a jointed spine that bends along the path it swims, with a whale-like kick that lifts and drops its tail (the fluke grows as it rises and fades under the surface as it falls); a long pointed snout and great toothed jaws that gape red as it locks on and lunges, a heavy banded barrel body, four rowing flippers and a shark-like tail. It is bigger (about three and a half tiles long), has 480 health instead of 320 and bites from farther. The long segmented creature it used to be stays in the sea as its own leviathan, the Undertow Leviathan, with the old look and stats. Ocean sections keep the leviathan they always rolled: those that rolled the Mosasaur's second place now hold the Undertow Leviathan.
- Release 227 — strange stones can be touched. Since release 215 only strange trees opened their Glimpse: the rules for ordinary rocks hid the Touch action on strange stones. Now any untouched strange tree or stone offers Touch first, at any Might; afterwards it is an ordinary rock (move, break or chip a stone).
- Release 225 — the shovel. Lash one together anywhere from 2 wood and 2 stone (Act facing a dungeon wall makes one if you carry none). Act facing a dungeon wall digs a block; a tunnel goes up to 3 blocks deep from open floor, and a shovel breaks after 6 blocks. Some wall blocks hide treasure (marks, Cinder Iron, Lumen Dust, rarely a Sphere); most give no sign, a few glint. A shovel makes shortcuts, never keys: it never digs a dungeon's outer edge, doors, sealed gates or anything standing in the way, never opens a sealed or guarded part early, and is never used in hunts, portals, dives or crossings. Tunnels stay dug. Stone: chip one from any rock (any Might), 2 from breaking a rock, 1 from every block dug. In the overworld a rare patch of disturbed earth can be dug once for a find.
- Release 223 — the hands, reworked. Each hand is now one painted silhouette instead of tubes joined together: fingers swell at the joints and end in rounded pads, the knuckle line arches, a web of skin joins thumb and index, and the light and shadow fall across the whole hand at once with a single rim of light around it. Fingers close in a wave from index to little finger and keep a slight living sway. The wagon's gloved fists are seen from above, low on the reins; the drowned hands claw. Also: in the Journal's Creatures section a creature's picture no longer stretches when you open its variants; it stays square and the list opens beside it. People in the Glimpses are held to the same standard: your ash hands in the Burning Plain and the hand pressed on the ice are painted hands; the woman at the table in the Fourth Floor and the people standing in the fire have real proportions instead of pawn shapes; and on the rock in the Plant your feet are no longer drawn, only the ropes running down to the stones. On the Atlas your waypoint and the quest marker now stay visible at every zoom: a pin at a fixed size on top of everything, and when one is off the map an arrow at the edge points to it with its distance in sections.
- Release 221 — painted hands. Every hand in the Glimpses (the wagon's gloved fists on the reins, the hand on the stair rail and the axe in The Fourth Floor, the drowned hands, and the hand reaching for the plant, now bare and warm-lit instead of gauntlet-dark) is now painted from one shared hand built like the snake: tapering bodies with soft ends and brushwork along the form, no outlines. Fingers bend in depth, so a fist reads as knuckles; the drowned hands claw.
- Release 219 — the first Glimpse from the legends: Gilgamesh, "The Plant at the Bottom". Night on a rock over the sea, stones roped to your feet; you drop through the dark to a thorny plant glowing on the sea floor, and its thorns open your hand. At dawn you lay it on a stone by a pool and bathe; a snake comes out of the reeds, takes it, and leaves its pale skin behind. It is painted in brushstrokes rather than hard shapes, as Tim asked.
- Release 217 — linked dungeons. About a third of the multi-level dungeons are linked: once you beat the guardian past the second level, its door lets you out somewhere far away and unexplored, at another mouth of the same dungeon. Either mouth enters it, and the guardian's door then leads to the other one. On the Atlas a dashed green line with green rings joins the two mouths (under Places).
- Release 215 — story beats live in strange things. About one section in thirty holds a tree, shrub or stone that is subtly wrong: a pale glow, a drifting double of itself, motes rising off it. Nothing marks them on the Atlas; you notice them or you don't. Touch one and a Glimpse opens. Afterwards it is ordinary. Felling trees no longer opens Glimpses at all, and ruins, shrines and crossings open them only rarely now, so you never have to chop or poke at everything to find the story.
- Release 213 — a cleaner Atlas.
  - **Places:** this layer now marks towns (Ember Refuge and the hamlets, with a gold house), crossings and other sites worth finding. Supply caches and shelters are no longer marked, and the separate Crossings toggle is folded back into Places.
  - **Wayglasses:** they keep their own toggle, so with All off and only Wayglasses on you see just the places you can travel to.
  - **Waypoint & death:** the old Waypoints toggle is renamed to say what it shows, your manual waypoint and where you last died.
  - **Travel:** tapping a section with a Wayglass chooses it for travel and brings the glowing Travel button into view, so you no longer have to scroll for it.
- Release 211 — where a shallow channel or a lake runs up to the edge of a section, the hard grey wall there is gone, so your skiff (or you, wading) can cross into the next section. Before, only deep sea opened a section's edge. This applies to sections you have already explored, unless they were first explored before release 104, whose terrain is kept exactly as it was.
- Release 209 — the Atlas layers can be picked apart. Wayglasses and Crossings (crossings, glass kilns and arenas) now have toggles of their own beside Other places, so you can switch everything else off and see only them, in the close view and in the world view. With Relays on, the relay pattern now stays visible as you zoom out: the land dims and the whole relay pattern next to where you have been shows in solid violet, so the fractal the relays make across the world reads as one shape. Answered relays and their links stay marked at every zoom.
- Release 207 — on a phone held sideways the Atlas's tool column scrolls again, so Stuck?, Return to Ember Refuge, Set waypoint and the legend can be reached, while the map stays in place. The world view also redraws faster while you pinch or drag a large journey. The "Under the Ice" Glimpse no longer stops on older browsers.
- Release 205 — the Atlas zooms out to the whole world.
  - **Zooming:** pinch, scroll the mouse wheel, use +/− (each press now scales the view rather than nudging it), or double-tap a section to zoom in on it. You can zoom out until hundreds of sections fit across.
  - **World view:** zoomed far out, every explored section is a small coloured square. The regions you have heard are tinted and named, and Wayglasses, towns, signals, your waypoint, the compass target, your last death and you are marked.
  - **World and Go to…:** World frames everything you have explored. Go to… jumps to the whole world, to where you are, to any region you have heard (framing all of it), or to a town, shelter or Wayglass inside it.
  - **Arrows:** they move a third of the view at any zoom.
  - **Sideways phones:** the Atlas fills the screen, with the map on the left and the tools in a column on the right. An empty "Discovered level" box no longer shows on the overworld.
- Release 203 — six Glimpses of horror and violence.
  - "The Fourth Floor": you climb a yellow stairwell to a door left ajar; a small figure turns away toward the lamp, and your hands lift an axe. A ring of keys keeps swaying long after.
  - "The Hunt": you are the predator, seeing in heat; the deer runs, you leap, and your claws come down.
  - "The Trench": flares at night, the man ahead turning to speak, a shell, and the world tipping over into ringing white.
  - "The Drowning": you sink while pale hands rise out of the dark and take hold.
  - "The Wheat": something keeps pace with you in the wheat; you stop, and it stops; you turn, and it stands up.
  - "The Burning Plain": barefoot on embers, your hands turning to ash, calm figures standing in the fire.

  The violence is shown in silhouette, light and red. These Glimpses join the same pools as the others.
- Release 201 — six more Glimpses, the first of the full suite. "The Sky": lying in a field, clouds drifting into the grid of the Atlas, a far cannon, your eyes closing. "Rain at the Window": a dark room, rain on the glass, someone's head on your shoulder, one flash of lightning. "Under the Ice": a fish looking up at boots crossing the ice until a hand presses flat above you. "The Spheres": one of many glowing spheres in an amber void, chiming when they touch. "Edge-Light": darkness, and a hairline of light along something vast. "Glyph Rain": falling ones and zeros, then wedges and letters of no alphabet, which briefly become a face. They join the first three in the same pools: human near Ember Refuge, unhuman farther out. All of them can be watched from Options → System or the Proving Ground's Glimpses button.
- Release 199 — a Glimpse now freezes the game completely while it plays. Before, switching away from the app during a Glimpse and coming back brought up the pause menu over it, and continuing from that menu let the game run underneath the Glimpse.
- Release 197 — the Elder Wanderers. Rare, tall, faceless creatures on four stilt legs walk the Corridor's roads (and, more rarely, wander inside crossings). They speak no language. Their bodies are made of a living pattern, and when you listen to one it answers with a pattern too: one of Stephen Wolfram's elementary cellular automaton rules, unfolding in the conversation box in its own colour. There are 88 essentially different rules, and each Elder speaks one. The Reciter's Tome gains "The Elder Wanderers · n of 88 patterns" with a small picture of every rule you have heard. A new Elder usually speaks a rule you have not heard yet, but the last few are hard to find. Elders keep moving: a road with none today may have one later. Hearing all 88 completes the recitation. Also fixed: watching a Glimpse again from the Tome or from Options no longer lets the game run underneath it, and previews return you to Options.
- Release 195 — the Proving Ground (and so every preview link) has a Glimpses button beside Diagnostics, which plays any of the Glimpses so they can be watched before a release is published.
- Release 193 — the first Glimpses: short first-person scenes, the Corridor seen for a few seconds through someone's, or something's, eyes. In "The Wagon at Dusk" you hold the reins through birches toward a setting sun while someone sleeps against your shoulder. In "The Lantern, Faceted" a moth flies to a lantern, seen through compound eyes, a hundred small pictures each a moment out of step. In "The Rule" your vision is a cellular automaton, Rule 30 and then Rule 110, and one amber cell keeping its own time is you. A Glimpse may open when you touch a stone, ruin or shrine (often), when you enter a crossing (sometimes) or when you fell a tree (rarely). Human scenes are likelier near Ember Refuge and stranger ones farther out. Each is drawn live by the game, with no image files. You will not see one again until you have seen them all, and never more than three times. The first sighting of each adds a fragment to the region you are in. Tap to let go of a Glimpse early. Seen Glimpses can be watched again in the Reciter's Tome, and Options → System → Glimpse previews plays any of them without counting. With reduced motion turned on they play as slow stills. A fragment found in a region you have not yet heard is no longer named, and the Tome lists only regions you have heard.
- Release 191 — the Corridor's regions now carry the residue of human lives: travellers and reciters speak passages from the books the Corridor still remembers, and each region has its own. Near Ember Refuge, in the Smoke Meadows, it is War and Peace. Farther out, each direction has its own region and book: the Ferry Reaches (Siddhartha) to the north, the Clear Country (Emerson) to the east, the Fevered Stairs (Crime and Punishment) to the south and the Whetstone Hills (Musashi) to the west. Beyond them lie the Long Table, the Autumn Galleries, the Low Rooms and the Ash Orchards, and past them all the Far Sleep. The old joke lines are gone, and nobody repeats a line you have already heard until everything they know has been said. Nothing tells you which regions or books exist: you learn you are somewhere new when someone there speaks a different book ("You are in the Ferry Reaches"), and from then on the Atlas names that region. The Reciter's Tome lists only what you have heard. It opens with "What the Corridor Remembers": each region holds its own number of fragments (from 13 to 33), shown as "The Smoke Meadows · 3 of 24 fragments", and certain passages you hear are fragments (the game tells you when). Scenes and words found in crossings will hold the rest in later releases, and when a region's last fragment is found, "Recitation complete" flashes across the screen and the region is marked Mastered. Stones, ruins, wrecks and lookouts now show their written lore when you touch them.
- Release 189 — swimming in a swamp or blight pool really no longer tires you (release 185 meant to stop it, but a slip left the stamina drain in place). The pools still lower your maximum health.

A sunken cache waits at the far end, and the surface line takes you back up to the buoy, aboard your skiff if you dove from it. If you drown, the line hauls you back to the buoy with fresh air and nothing lost.
- Release 106 — leviathans, the fourth stage of the water world. Out in the deep ocean, roughly one in four big stretches of deep water now holds a single giant. The Greywhale drifts peacefully until you strike it; then it sounds beneath you, its vast shadow spreads, and it breaches, throwing you back. The Tyrant Mosasaur stalks below with only its shadow showing, marks its lunge, tears across the surface, and then lies exposed for a moment. Three krakens: the Reef Kraken marks rings in the water and tentacles burst up through them (each limb can be cut down, never more than three at once); the Abyssal Kraken marks you, then seizes and drags you toward its maw until heavy blows break its grip; the Stormkraken raises a tentacle at a time and clouds the sea with ink. The first time you fell each leviathan you earn a trophy of 40 marks and a weapon sphere. All five have bestiary entries.
- Release 105 — sea creatures, the third stage of the water world. Seas, lakes and swamps you have not yet visited now hold creatures that live only in the water and warn before every strike. Lanceeels hide, show a pale wake line, then charge straight along it (step off the line). Ringcoil Serpents circle you, tightening until they bite; hitting one throws the coil wide. Sinewake Serpents weave toward you in an S with a long trailing body, bite, then back off. Breachfins mark a dark landing shadow and leap out in an arc; they can only be hit in the air. In the fens, Mire Leeches hop through the bog water and Bog Lurkers wait unseen, then bite and drag you a step closer. Hits taken aboard the skiff are shared with its hull. Every creature has a bestiary entry. Fix: resuming a save while aboard the skiff no longer moves you to shore.
- Release 104 — the Skiff, the second stage of the water world. Coasts and lakes you have not yet visited can hold a dock: a Shipwright on the shore beside a short plank pier. Once you hold the Tidemantle she sells a skiff for 120 marks and 2 cinder iron. Board at the berth and sail the sea, deep ocean, lakes, swamp pools and shallows at 1.4x walking speed with no stamina cost; the skiff has momentum, and a dodge is a quick oar burst. Sail into dry land to step ashore; the skiff stays moored where you left it, and you board it again from the shore. The skiff has its own hull (60): hits taken aboard are split with it, and if the hull breaks the skiff sinks, you swim, and the Shipwright's crew returns it to its dock, where she patches it for marks. Blight water and rivers cannot be sailed.
- Release 103 — Waterbodies, the first stage of the water world. Sections you have never visited can now hold true seas that span dozens of sections, with deep open water (swimmable with the Tidemantle, but it tires you twice as fast), scattered islands whose land follows the biome around them, and an island landmark (Tide Shrine, Wrecked Hull or Drowned Beacon) in each stretch of sea. Inland there are lakes, swamps (Sunken Fen: bog ground, slow mire, deep pools) and poison swamps (Blight Mire): wading or swimming in its green murk rots you, lowering your maximum health until you rest at a Wayglass or rest point. Sections you have already explored keep their terrain exactly.
- Release 102 — the Tidemantle, stage one of water traversal. Fell the guardian of a flooded dungeon (one with water in its halls) or a Mire Apostle and you earn the Tidemantle once: from then on rivers, flooded halls and open sea are swimmable at a little over half speed. Swimming stops stamina regeneration and drains it; an exhausted swimmer takes damage until they reach shore. There is no diving: lava, pits, chasms and canyons stay lethal, and without the Tidemantle deep water still drowns.
- Release 101 — falls are real again: walking into a chasm, lava, a river or off a cliff of four or more levels kills (the release-97 edge guard is gone; rims, fog hatching and level marks keep hazards readable). Deep ocean water is now enterable and drowns you; shallows stay walkable. Every landscape section and themed dungeon with one-level steps gets walkable ramps between levels (about one per short run, at least one each). Controls: Jump moves to the centre of the action diamond and the compass takes Jump's old place. Leech heals more (12% weapon, 8% tool, 5% spell; Blood Tide doubles it) and every heal shows a green +N over the Wayfarer.
- Release 100 — the Aperture Constellation and Awakenings complete the itemization rework. Disciplines become five branches (Form, Motion, Veil, Force and the new Rift with Rift Step, a ledge-safe blink), each with its active's ranks, a modifier and a choice of two capstones (Colossus or Cinder Heart; Aerial Step or Slipstream; Sanctuary or Mirror Veil; Amplifier or Resonant Force; Fold or Anchor). Points come from Aperture, one per 6; ranks and modifiers cost 1, capstones 2. Everything learned before this release is kept as bonus points, and a learned Aerial Step becomes the Motion capstone. Shrines gain Attune, the only place to respec (lumen dust). At Sela's forge a max-rank weapon holding a Resonance can be Awakened for 6 lumen dust: awakened weapons release a piercing crescent every third swing; awakened tools fan every shot into three. Forge lab scenario with everything ready.
- Release 99 — Creature Echoes, itemization part two. Every pack creature has an Echo of its signature move (15). Guardians always leave theirs the first time, other pack kills about one in twenty-five. Echoes cost 1-3 of three notches and trigger from what you already do, with no new buttons: dodges (Thunderhorn's Charge tramples, Lynx Pounce empowers the next strike, Pallid Skitter saves stamina, Spinner's Web slows, Eel's Phase extends invulnerability), landing a jump (Sunderjaw's Rupture), kills (Maw's Hunger pulls, Flesh Bloom slows, Seraph's Firefall ignites), being hurt (Choir's Lament strikes back, Mirror Swap trades places), casting (Wraith Lantern beam), firing (Beetle Mortar every third shot), facing foes (Saint's Regard slows them) and standing still (Lantern Stillness mends). New Echoes screen in the Pack; Forge lab Echoes sandbox.
- Release 98 — Glyphs & Resonances, the first part of the itemization rework. Ten glyphs (Ignite, Chain, Pierce, Echo, Leech, Heavy, Swift, Volatile, Tether, Still) set into weapon sockets (1-3 by tier) and two sockets per spell; the same glyph does something different in a weapon, a tool or a spell. Eight Resonances light when the right pair shares a host (Ember Choir, Wildfire, Storm Lattice, Afterimage, Grave Tether, Comet, Blood Tide, Quickstep) and are recorded the first time they fire. Guardians always drop a glyph, other kills about one in eight; salvage yields glyph dust and returns socketed glyphs; 6 dust inscribes any glyph you have found. Every save gets Ignite, Chain and Echo once. New Glyphs screen (Pack and Sela's Forge) and a Glyph Forge lab in the Proving Ground.
- Release 97 — traversal you can trust. Every raised level now looks the same everywhere (overworld, dungeons, Underlands): a lighter wash per level, a coloured trim (bronze 1, teal 2, gold 3, violet 4, white 5) and that many carved notches; ground stays plain, and the area label shows GROUND or LEVEL N. Beside you, cyan chevrons mark a ledge you can jump up to and red crosses one that is too high. Generation now guarantees every floor you can reach leads back out (no more trap holes: 24% of themed dungeons and about 10% of landscape sections had one). Pits, lava and deep water get a crumbling lit rim, the chasm is indigo instead of black, and unexplored fog is a hatched slate; walking or jumping stops at the edge of a pit or a fatal (4+ level) drop, and only a dodge roll carries you over. New Atlas button "Stuck? Get me out": dungeons return you to the level's entrance, the overworld to the nearest ground that leads out (free, 30 s cooldown).
- Release 96 — Underlands: new crossings use generator 5 with six layout grammars (spine, hub, ring, cavern, crypt, descent) and, in about 45% of them, a bespoke underground environment (Sunless Chasm with climbable ledges and a bottomless abyss, Lava Bog with drifting embers, Underground River with bridges and dripping water, Frozen Grotto with falling snow, Bone Crypt, Fungal Hollow with spores). Four new dungeon-only grotesques (Weeping Saint, Amalgam with its Pallid Crawler brood, Hanged Choir, Gnawing Maw) and Sunderjaw now live only underground. About a third of new crossings hold a Dormant Relay beside their guardian: restore or sever it to hear the far transmission; five answered relays wake the network. Elevation is easier to read (drop stripes, slope chevrons, a JUMP / TOO HIGH ledge marker). Crossings entered before this release keep their layout, population and story state. New Underlands & Relay Proving Ground lab.
- Release 95 — the Proving Ground developer menu collapses to a small "Dev menu" chip (Hide button, or the backtick key) so the game is playable on a phone; the choice is remembered on the device.
- Release 94 — Landscape Pack: overland geography from continuous world-scale noise, so ranges, deserts and coasts span sections. About two in five sections carry a feature: Snowpeak Ranges (elevation up to level 5, snow, crags, stair trails to a Summit Cairn, sky bridges between high plateaus, and sometimes an erupting volcano with a fatal lava crater and lava bombs), Dune Seas (walkable dune faces, mesas), Shattered Coasts (ocean, beaches, shallows causeways), Lumen Wilds (glowing moss, crystal spires, jump-only floating platforms) and rare walled Overland Castles (gate, courtyard, stairs to walkable battlements, keep, lookout). Elevation is real: the player climbs one level per jump and may drop off any ledge (one level safe, two and three hurt, four or more fatal); ground creatures change level only on stairs and slopes. Every walkable tile can be climbed to from the plaza. High-detail cached procedural textures with pseudo-3D cliff faces; the atlas colours landscape sections. Sections explored before release 94, the starting ring and settlements keep their terrain. Proving Ground Landscape lab with every biome, an erupting volcano and a sky bridge.
- Release 93 — the Dungeon Monster Pack roams the overworld: every section except the starting one respawns with terrain-matched pack creatures (about 45% of ordinary hostiles converted, plus 1 extra pack resident near the origin and 3 from four sections out), raising overland density by roughly two thirds. Wildlife, shelter and district residents, story and elite creatures are unchanged. Pack creatures respect settlement sanctuaries: they never spawn inside, are pushed out, pause while the player is inside, and cannot hurt the player there.
- Release 92 — Dungeon Monster Pack: ten new composable creatures (Lumen Wraith, Sunderjaw, Grave Lantern, Rift Lynx, Siege Beetle, Veil Spinner, Strata Eel, Thunderhorn, Mirror Shade, Cinder Seraph) built from shared movement, AI-brain and attack-module tables, with high-detail animated canvas art. New mechanics: wall-phasing, flight, burrowing (untargetable while underground), blinking, persistent sweeping beams, stillness-punishing floor curses, lobbed mortars that lead a moving target, slowing webs, leap slams, wall-stunning ram charges, jumpable shockwaves, harmless mirror clones and dive bombs. About a fifth of individuals graft one borrowed attack. Every pack creature has a boss form whose extra attacks unlock at 66% and 33% health; ordinary dungeons now draw their guardian from an 11-creature pool instead of always the Hollow Marshal. Never-entered dungeons are slightly denser (+2 ordinary, +3 deep, active cap 8/10) and mix pack residents into each ecology; previously entered dungeons and the Missing Crossing story dungeon keep their original population and guardian. The Proving Ground combat lab lists every pack creature plus `pack-boss-<id>` scenarios and a "Near the combatants" teleport; the journal bestiary shows each creature with generated field art.
