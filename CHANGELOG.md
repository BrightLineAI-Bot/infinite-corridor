# Changelog

## Unreleased

- Release 337 — Echoes from every first kill.
  - **Guardians:** each of the 16 named guardian creatures now always leaves its Echo the first time you beat it, if you don't already have that Echo. That covers guardians you've beaten before without getting one. Before, it was a 3% chance.
  - **World beasts and the Questing Beast:** the same rule; their first kill already gave the Echo.
  - **Earlier kills:** a Questing Beast (or world beast) you killed in the past without getting its Echo now grants that Echo when you load the game.
  - **Still no Echo:** the plain guardians (Hollow Marshal and the Deep wardens) and ordinary creatures.

- Release 335 — glyphs, buffs and presets checked and fixed. Every armor glyph, skill glyph, weapon glyph, resonance, spell pairing and constellation passive was tested in play; most already worked. Fixed:
  - **Armor Volatile and Ignite** now scale the whole hit: +10% to each melee blow, and +12% to each spell and magic shot. Before, they only boosted a small flat bonus. Ignite no longer boosts physical tool shots, and Volatile no longer makes the world beasts stronger.
  - **Rift Step** now recovers faster with armor Chain and with a cooldown pairing (Eclipse, Hollow and the like), like every other skill.
  - **A spell's lingering pulses keep their own glyphs.** Before, casting your other spell swapped the glyphs on pulses already burning. A Leech Ember Ring stopped healing the moment you cast Cinder Dart.
  - **The Arcanum follows the active preset.** The Spells panel shows the active preset's spell, not the last one cast. The Glyphs tab shows sockets for exactly what preset 1 or 2 casts: A, B and a spell or skill on its Build button. A preset with no spell no longer shows an old one.

- Release 333 — the larger Threefold Deep's final gate opens again. In the larger Threefold Deep, the corridor behind the final gate stopped one row short of the Sealed Choir. With all three wardens beaten, the gate said it was open but led into a wall. The corridor now runs on into the Choir.
  - **Deeps already found:** they are fixed too, including ones you're partway through. Nothing else in them changes, except that the final boss may stand somewhere else in its chamber.

- Release 331 — world bosses catch up with each Listener.
  - **Before Infinite III:** they still match your strength.
  - **At Infinite III:** they hold at the strength they had then, and stay there until you've beaten 3 Listeners, as before.
  - **From the third Listener on:** each Listener you beat catches the world bosses (and the Questing Beast and the roaming Gate Revenant) up to your strength at that moment. They then hold there until you beat the next one.
  - **You set the pace:** the time between Listeners is yours to grow stronger than them.

- Release 329 — a pause after a world boss fells you. When a world beast or the Questing Beast kills the Wayfarer, they lie fallen for 4 seconds before recovering at the checkpoint as usual. Meanwhile the beast calms down and paces around where it stood, and its shots fade. Other deaths recover at once, as before.

- Release 327 — the game's sound is about 30 dB louder and brighter. All of it, the music and the combat pulse, was mixed very quietly, around −50 dB. It now sits near a normal game level (about −20 dB average) and is less muffled. The Master and Music sliders in Options → Audio still scale it.

- Release 325 — a Threefold Deep always opens all the way, with all three wings and their wardens. One entered below its tier (a taste) keeps its full size, but its creatures are scaled to you:
  - **Scaling:** 55% of their usual health and damage at the start, and 5% more for each Aperture band you had reached when you first went in.
  - **Same for the rest:** the deep archetypes and world-threat arenas entered early work the same way.

- Release 323 — no dungeon is ever sealed. Every Threefold Deep, deep archetype and world-threat arena can be entered whatever your Aperture.
  - **Below its tier:** the dungeon opens as its taste. A Threefold Deep wakes only one wing; the deep archetypes and arenas open whole.
  - **At or above its tier:** it opens whole.
  - **Fixed once entered:** the version is decided the first time you go in, and stays.

- Release 321 — the drone. From the fourth Gate (4 Listeners), Pack → Supplies lets you choose what the frame calls up on land: **Ashore: Robot** or **Ashore: Drone**.
  - **Look.** A small silver drone with a blue eye, two rotors and a rocket pod.
  - **Rockets.** Every 1.6 s it fires a homing rocket at the nearest creature within 8 tiles.
  - **Mending.** Press the frame's button while hurt and it mends 30% of your health; it also does this by itself when you drop below 30%. Then it rests 30 s; a green bar under you shows when it's ready.
  - **Carrying.** Jump, then hold Jump: it grips your shoulders and carries you for up to 3 s (blue bar), over chasms, water and lava.
  - **Calling it.** Press the button again to dock it. Nothing can hit it.

- Release 319 — the dungeon ladder. The special dungeons now arrive in steps as your Aperture opens:
  - **Dormant and Stirring (tiers 0–1): the base game.**
    - Ordinary crossings, short plain dungeons, the six original environments and five of the sixteen guardians.
    - Now and then a large crossing, kept plain: no environment and no daises.
  - **From Open (tier 2): tastes.** Each special kind not yet unlocked can still turn up about a quarter as often, and smaller where a smaller version exists:
    - a one-room Poison Crypt;
    - a Threefold Deep with only one wing awake, whose warden alone opens the final gate;
    - a Revenant horde of 12–18.
  - **Unlocks:**
    - **Resonant (tier 3):** large dungeons and Watcher's daises, the newer environments, ten guardians and the roaming Gate Revenant.
    - **Tier 6:** all sixteen guardians, Poison Crypts, world-threat arenas and the Threefold Deep.
    - **Tier 9:** the deep archetypes and the Revenant horde.
  - **How often.** An unlocked kind starts at half of its usual rate and climbs to 80% by Infinite II. Infinite III has everything at full rate.
  - **Sealed entrances.** A deep or arena entrance that hasn't opened for you says which band it answers to. One you have entered stays open.
  - **Saved dungeons are safe.** Each dungeon keeps the tier it was first found at, and dungeons found before this release are unchanged.
  - **Robot fix.** After the boost runs dry, it waits for you to let go of Jump before firing again, so the fuel refills properly.

- Release 317 — the robot ashore. From the Infinite III band, with the second Gate unlocked (2 Listeners), the submersible frame works on land too:
  - **Calling it.** Use the frame on land (from Pack or a preset button). The first time, one frame is spent to build the robot. After that the same button calls it up and folds it away. Aboard the skiff, the frame still fits the submersible as before.
  - **Defence.** The robot has 60 plating in its own silver bar under the Wayfarer, which takes hits first. You also take 10% less damage while inside.
  - **Plating.** If the plating breaks, the robot folds away and can be called again after 20 seconds. Folded away, its plating mends at 3 a second.
  - **Boosting.** Jump, then hold Jump to fire the back boosters. This gives up to 1.6 seconds of flight, shown in a blue bar, over chasms, water and lava. The bar refills on the ground.
  - **Everything else still works.** Rift Step, spells, skills, attacks and items all still work inside the robot.

- Release 315 — the submersible becomes a robot, and diving is unlocked step by step:
  - **The robot.** The submersible is now a small silver robot, a little larger than the Wayfarer: a big round viewport where you can see them, short legs, two robotic arms with three-fingered hands, and boosters on its back. At sea it sits half in the water.
  - **Diving with the rig and Lens.** This needs the Infinite II band of the Aperture. Before that the Shipwright does not offer the dive rig and the buoys only show what they need. The skiff and the Tidemantle are unchanged.
  - **The submersible.** This also needs Infinite II, plus the Void Walker or a Listener defeated.
  - **Under the sea by submersible.** Diving at buoys and riding the deep current to the next buoy needs Infinite III, plus both the Void Walker and a Listener defeated.
  - **Messages.** Each locked step tells you what is still missing.

- Release 313 — a skill's button now shows how long its effect has left. While Ember Form, Quickening, Aerial Step, Veil Guard or Force Channel is running (or Rift Step's glyphs are riding on your hits), its button glows gold, and the gold drains away from the left as the effect runs out. The cooldown number still counts down as before.

- Release 311 — the submersible:
  - **Forging and fitting.** Forge a Submersible Frame from 3 cinder iron in Pack → Supplies, then use it aboard your skiff. Silver plates close over the boat, with a great round window at the prow where you can see the Wayfarer, and manipulator arms on the side.
  - **What it gives you.** 40 more hull in its own silver bar, which takes hits first, and 5% less damage to you while aboard.
  - **Diving.** Dodge aboard to dive under the surface for about a second, untouchable. Only one arm breaks the water while you are under.
  - **Dive buoys.** A submersible can dive at the red-and-white buoys with no rig or lens. In each grotto's lair a deep current now runs on: follow it and you come up beside the next buoy along.
  - **Repairs and presets.** Another frame mends the plating. The frame can go on any preset slot like other items.
  - **Sinking.** A sunk submersible comes back a plain skiff.

- Release 309 — targeting and free slots:
  - **Lock on.** Tap a creature to lock on to it (red brackets mark it). Until you tap it again, tap another creature, or it dies or gets far away, your swings go at it whenever it is in reach, and your tool shots and spells aim at it. Rift Step still goes where you are moving.
  - **Any slot, anything.** Spell A, Spell B and the Build button can each hold a spell or discipline, Build, or the preset's item. Set A or B to Build or Item in Arcanum → Spell slots; put a spell on the Build button with "Set on Build button" beside it. Choose the preset's item in Pack → Supplies, as before.

- Release 307 — world bosses are fairer:
  - **Health and damage.** Every world beast (and the Questing Beast and the Poison Crypt's Mire Heart) has half the health it had, and deals 10% less damage.
  - **Four tries.** You now get four tries at each before it moves on, instead of three.
  - **The Morphing Blob** no longer heals as it grows. It still swells, and its bloblets still heal it a little when they make it home.
  - **Staggers.** Every boss can now be staggered. Land enough blows and it breaks: it stops what it was doing and stands dazed for a couple of seconds, swaying, with stars circling over it. The first break comes fairly quickly, the second takes more blows, and from the third on every break takes the same, larger number. After it recovers there is a cooldown before your blows count again. The Mountain Turtle and the Lightning Giant are the hardest to break and the Void Walker is sturdy; the Astral Worm breaks surprisingly easily. The Psychic Crystal keeps its own break.

- Release 305 — the Far Sleep made strange. Past the farthest Readings, more than 35 sections out, the ground stops pretending:
  - pale sleep-meadow scattered with small luminous flowers;
  - white marble terraces climbing in wide stairs;
  - standing sleeper-stones with closed eyes;
  - patches where the ground has worn so thin that the night sky shows through (you cannot walk there, but it never kills you).

  Pale petals drift down. At sea only the islands change. The first time you step into it, the game tells you. Only ground you have not explored yet changes.

- Release 303 — the Poison Crypt, a rare new dungeon (about 1 in 50 new crossings):
  - Sealed crypt halls sunk in wide stretches of poison mire, with green gas jets along the way that burn and poison.
  - In the far corner lies the lair of the Mire Heart: the Morphing Blob at half the strength of its overland self, waiting in a great pool of mire.
  - Until you find the lair, the mire's pools keep bubbling up seeplings: small pieces of the Blob that grow as they go and crawl home to it. Each one that reaches the Heart makes it stronger. They burn and poison you if you touch them, so kill them on the way.
  - Once you reach the lair, the pools go still.

- Release 301 — more variety in new dungeons:
  - **Short plain dungeons.** About one new dungeon in four is now a small box of three to five plain rooms joined by a corridor or two: the entrance, the guardian's room, and little else.
  - **Special environments are rarer.** Most dungeons now have the ordinary look. About three in ten have a special environment (crypt, glacier, fungal hollow and the rest), and the five newest (Starfall Hive, Geode Hollow, Mosaic Sanctum, Rootbound Deep, Brass Engine) come up half as often as the others.
  - **Watcher's daises.** Some of the largest dungeons hold two stone daises bearing the Watcher's sigil, far apart. Touch one and its sigil wakes and looks for its twin. Once you have found and touched both, touching either one carries you to the other.

  Dungeons you have already entered are unchanged.

- Release 299 — five new dungeon environments, each with its own floor, decorations and air. They appear only in dungeons you have not entered yet, and more dungeons now get a special environment (about 6 in 10, up from about 4 in 10):
  - **Starfall Hive:** violet alien moss, still teal pools, crystal spires, and hexagonal platforms one jump up.
  - **Geode Hollow:** the inside of a split stone, with amethyst clusters that catch the light.
  - **Mosaic Sanctum:** a temple floor of large inlaid rosettes, rows of columns and still pools, lit from above.
  - **Rootbound Deep:** mossy earth with great knotted roots winding across the floor.
  - **Brass Engine:** iron grating over huge buried gears, with pipes that still breathe steam.

- Release 297 — glyphs now work with Aperture skills. A skill set in Spell A or B (Rift Step, Aerial Step and the rest) has two glyph sockets of its own on the Arcanum page:
  - Swift: recovers 25% faster.
  - Heavy: lasts 35% longer; Rift Step goes one tile further.
  - Echo: lasts 25% longer; Rift Step gains a charge.
  - Pierce: recovers 10% faster; Rift Step goes two tiles further.
  - Ignite, Chain, Leech, Volatile, Tether and Still ride on your weapon and tool hits while the skill is active, or for 4 seconds after a Rift Step.

- Release 295 — armour now has glyph sockets (one to three by tier), and the glyphs you already have give it steady bonuses:
  - Swift: move speed
  - Heavy: damage reduction
  - Leech: maximum health
  - Echo: longer Aperture skills
  - Chain: faster spell and skill recovery
  - Pierce: faster attacks and shots
  - Tether: faster stamina return
  - Still: cheaper dodges
  - Ignite: magic damage
  - Volatile: melee damage

  Bonuses of the same kind add up, to a cap. Each weapon, tool and armour card in Glyphs has "Save layout", and "Apply saved layout" puts the same glyphs onto new gear from your pouch. The Hull Shield is no longer offered for presets: Build already raises it at sea.

- Release 293 — Options → Accessibility has a Child-safe Glimpses switch. With it on, the horror and violence Glimpses never appear, and they are hidden from the Library and the previews; creatures are unchanged. Turn it off and they come back. Items now have their own drawn icons, on the Build button and in Supplies: a bark shield for Ironbark Tonic, a glowing vial for Lumen Phial, a green ampoule with roots for Clearroot, and a shielded hull for the Hull Shield. The Restorative Draught is no longer offered for presets, since it has its own Potion button.

- Release 291 — presets and the back button:
  - **Presets.** Each spell preset now also chooses what the Build button does: Build, or Item. Pick the item in Pack → Supplies: each item has Preset 1 and Preset 2 buttons, and only a preset set to Item can take one. In Item mode the button shows the item and how many you have, and pressing it uses one.
  - **Back button.** The phone's back button now reliably opens the pause menu during play and closes it again. Before, the first press could leave the app, and pressing back on the pause menu left the game frozen.
  - **Crossing Sigils** are listed in Supplies again.

- Release 289 — the Watcher's sigil appears in the world:
  - Some dungeons have it inlaid in the floor, and arenas carry it as a large mosaic with an ornate border.
  - Rarely, out in the world, you will find a shrine ruin whose raised platforms form the sigil, each part one jump above the last.
  - On the shrine's eye, and very rarely in a shelter or beside a crypt's coffins, lies a pristine Watcher's tomb: its sigil bright, a halo above it. Touch it once and it folds you elsewhere: from the world, across the corridor through a pocket crossing; underground, to a far corner of the same dungeon.
  - These appear only in sections and dungeons you have not yet explored.
  - The Psychic Crystal's first dodge after it rises from a stagger now waits its full 3 seconds.

- Release 287 — building and dungeons:
  - **Building.** Blocks make stairs: stand on a block, stack two beside it, and jump on up to a level-3 ledge. You can have five builds; a sixth replaces the oldest (a stack's lower block goes first and the top one stays). Two blocks stacked, or a wall, stop flying creatures that are not charging, and the Psychic Crystal can no longer drift through your builds.
  - **The Psychic Crystal** keeps to itself until you touch it or attack it.
  - **Dungeon supply caches** now give 2 stone and 2 wood. On deeper levels some give 3 and 3.
  - **Menus.** Supplies shows one small line of counts with a dot between each, and your shovel's digs beneath it. The Build options sit under "Build with", and the chosen one is gold, the same gold that now marks the selected tab or toggle everywhere, Journal tabs included. When you cannot afford any build, the Build button shows a red barred circle over the hammer.
  - **Crypt coffins** now come in four looks (clean, weathered, cracked, and an opened empty tomb), with the Watcher's sigil carved more quietly.

- Release 285 — the Psychic Crystal comes out to fight. Once it has engaged you, it can spend only about one second in every five inside walls or over ground you cannot reach; when that runs out it leaves at once, even mid-attack. It dodges far less often (every 3 seconds at most, was about 1), takes 14 hits to stagger (was 10), and cannot be staggered again for 10 seconds after it rises.

- Release 283 — dungeon water is dangerous now. Swimming it costs 5% of your max health every second (your health, not your maximum), and going into it, or staying in it, with no stamina drowns you on the spot. Water outside dungeons is unchanged. Rot from poison and bog pools now shows as a grey band at the end of your health bar, the share of your health it has taken, instead of an empty one. The Bone Crypt coffins now bear an arcane sigil (an eye within a ring, circled by an orbit and its moon, under a crescent, above a small constellation) instead of a cross. Pack → Supplies leads with clear tiles for wood, stone, Cinder Iron and shovel digs, and every recipe (walls, blocks, shovel, skiff) sits together under Recipes. A new Continuous build checkbox moves on to the next material when your chosen one runs out. The Build button names the material it will use, shows REPAIR beside a damaged skiff, and is crossed out when you cannot afford any build.

- Release 281 — every action button lights up the instant you touch it, and taps in the gaps around Dodge reach it more readily (Dodge still needs stamina). The diagnostic probe now records what became of every Dodge press. The Leech glyph now heals at least a share of your max health per hit, so it shows at high level. A stray ">" near the action buttons is gone.

- Release 279 — the compass keeps what you chose. The target you step the compass to, and a quest you point it at from the Journal, are now kept in your save, so closing the Atlas, reloading or wandering far no longer changes them. Opening the Atlas no longer arms "Set manual waypoint" on the section you are standing in (tap a section first), and the tap that closes the Atlas or a panel can no longer land on the compass beneath it.
- Release 279 — Proving Ground: a Standard Test Tile laboratory, one fixed section with a pond, a chasm, a two-level plateau with stairs, a building and a dungeon door (which leads into a real dungeon, and the tile is put back as it was when you come out). The Monster & Elite Combat Arena now has an Environment list (the test tile, the three dungeon recipes, the eight arena families and every overworld biome) instead of needing a typed seed, and nothing starts until you press Start test.

- Release 277 — a supply cache now gives a stone, a wood and a restorative draught along with its usual supply (one draught in all if its supply is a draught; it gave 2 stone and 1 wood in 275).
- Release 277 — a Broken Observatory holds a cache: the first time you inspect one it gives 3 wood, 3 stone, or a stone and a restorative draught (which one is fixed for each observatory).

- Release 275 — supply caches now also give 2 stone and 1 wood, on top of their supplies.
- Release 275 — gathering stone fixed. With Might 3 or more, Act (and Tool) on a rock used to roll it aside first, giving no stone and leaving nothing to chip. Now the first press chips the rock's stone (every rock gives one), the next breaks it into rubble (breaking no longer adds stone), and only then can it be rolled aside. Rubble and rolled-aside rocks no longer catch your presses, so Act goes to the next rock.
- Release 275 — creature stages. Every creature so far is Stage 1, and Stage 1 runs to Aperture tier 12. Your Aperture now stops one point short of tier 13 until you fell the Listener at the Far Gate; the first Listener opens Stage 2 (up to tier 24), and every two more open the next stage (tier 36 after the third, and so on). A save already past its stage's top tier keeps its Aperture but gains no more until the Listener falls. The HUD shows your stage beside the tier (S1), and the Aperture card says when the stage is holding you.
- Release 275 — Stage 1 world beasts and the Gate Revenant stop growing. Until now world beasts, the Questing Beast and the Gate Revenant always matched your strength. From Aperture tier 13 (Stage 2) they are held at the strength they had when you got there, so you outgrow them, until you have felled the Listener three times; then they match you again. Every other creature and boss keeps growing as before.

- Release 273 — world beasts come with your Aperture. A world beast is met for the first time only once your Aperture reaches its tier: the Mountain Turtle, the Void Walker and the Psychic Crystal from tier 3 (Resonant), the Morphing Blob from tier 4, the Lightning Giant from tier 5, the Astral Worm from tier 6, the Nanobot Knight and the Tremor Worm from tier 7, and the Questing Beast last, from tier 8. Below tier 3 none appear. A beast you have already met (or are tracking) stays where it is.
- Release 273 — a Gate Revenant horde now gives Crossing Sigils: its count is split into four waves, and the kill that ends each wave leaves a Sigil (four in all, the last with the Gate piece). Ordinary Gate Revenants still leave one each.

- Release 271 — Gate Revenants stalk, then hunt. In a dungeon a Gate Revenant keeps 5 to 8 tiles off at first, slipping through the walls to stay near you, and turns on you at once if you come within a few tiles or wound it. Two minutes after you enter a dungeon they all hunt you wherever you are. Hunting, they pass straight through dungeon walls, never out into the black beyond, and one left inside rock steps out onto floor you can reach.
- Release 271 — Gate Revenants fight smarter. A Revenant may slip a swing or a shot about to land, side-stepping in a blink (not every time, and not again for a couple of seconds). Land 8 blows on one inside 3 seconds and its poise breaks: it sinks to one knee, open and still, for 2.4 seconds, then springs away.
- Release 271 — the Gate Revenant has a new look: a hooded, horned wraith in a tattered cloak with the Gate's arch burning on its chest. It looks faded while it stalks, its eyes flare red on the hunt and brighter as it winds up, it leaves an afterimage when it dodges, and it kneels while its poise is broken. It costs no more to draw than before.
- Release 271 — the Gate Revenant's true face. In its long lava barrage, the one where it plants itself and lobs a stream of lava balls that arc and burst, its cape flies back into ragged wings and its chest splits open into one huge gaping mouth, rows of teeth and four great curved canines, lava glowing in its throat, spitting embers. It opens as the barrage winds up and closes as it recovers.

- Release 269 — the Gate Revenant horde. About one new dungeon in two hundred holds nothing but Gate Revenants, 28 to 44 of them. Three stand at a time, and as each falls another comes after a few seconds, until the dungeon's count is spent. Any you've drawn follow you out of the dungeon and across sections. A kill anywhere counts down the dungeon's number, which is kept in your save: if you die or lose them, the survivors go back and wait. Breaking the horde locks a piece of the Far Gate into place. Horde Revenants don't drop Crossing Sigils.
- Release 269 — building costs less: a wood wall is 4 wood, a stone block 5 stone.
- Release 269 — Build patches your skiff. Beside your damaged skiff or aboard it (a REPAIR tag shows), each press of Build spends a wood and mends half the hull.
- Release 269 — the hull guard. Holding Build at sea deploys a hull shield you carry. For up to 3 minutes the skiff becomes a Viking longboat, seen three-quarters on: a striped square sail, shields along its side, and a lantern hanging from its curled prow with a small spiral galaxy behind the glass (in fog it lights the mist violet). The hull takes half damage, a fuchsia guard bar beside the hull gauge soaks blows first (the guard ends when it's gone), and you take 5% less.

- Release 267 — build mode. Pressing Build still builds in front of you, and now also turns on build mode (the button lights up, like Tool's aiming). In build mode, a tap on the map builds exactly where you tap if it's within 3 tiles, or 3 tiles out along the same line if it's farther. A fence there is turned across the line from you to the tap, straight or diagonal, in 8 directions. Act, Tool or Attack switch back.
- Release 267 — a wood wall is never built where any part of you stands (it used to be possible to wall yourself in for its 10 seconds), and builds are cleared when you enter a dungeon.

- Release 265 — the Potion button shows a red flask instead of the word, with the count beneath it.
- Release 265 — building. A Build button with a drawn claw hammer (and WOOD or STONE beneath it) sits in the action cluster's lower-left corner (Spell A's old place); choose wood or stone in the Pack's Supplies tab. It raises a build on the tile in front of you. A wood wall (5 wood) blocks the way and takes one blow; built while moving sideways it runs up and down across your way, otherwise it stands across as usual. A stone block (10 stone) takes two blows, and only you can stand on it: jump onto it like a ledge; creatures can't climb it. Stack two to make a step. Builds crumble after 10 seconds, at most 5 stand at once, and none go on water, chasms or anything already built. A build between a creature and you takes the hit instead of you. A world beast's charge or dash goes straight through, and its close blows smash a build while stopping the damage.
- Release 265 — the Refuge's lands. A farm with big trees and a quarry with a stone block's worth of rock lie near Ember Refuge, apart from each other. Shale, the quarry keeper, a small stone turtle in a straw hat, tells you plainly how it works; stone is still never sold. Hollis Dray, the Refuge Farmer, says where they are, and other, stranger things.
- Release 265 — regrowth. Every 20 new sections you discover, felled trees and worked rocks grow back. The farm and the quarry come first, then everything within a ring around the Refuge that widens by a section each time (up to 5). Trees felled across a gap as bridges stay down.

- Release 263 — Echoes drop again. A bug meant no kill ever left one: live pack creatures carry a "pack creature" flag where the drop code expected the creature's name, so every drop missed. Echoes now come from guardians, world beasts and the Questing Beast (a world beast's first kill gives its Echo for certain), as a 3% chance at that creature's Echo while you don't have it. Ordinary creatures no longer drop them. The Pallid Crawler joins the creatures that can guard a dungeon, so every creature with an Echo can be met as a guardian or a world beast; dungeons you've already entered keep their guardian.
- Release 263 — Crossing Sigils drop. The first time you kill each world beast (and the Questing Beast) from this release on, it leaves a Crossing Sigil and its Echo for certain; after that, each kill has a 20% chance at a Sigil. The Gate Revenant leaves one every time. A guardian on a dungeon's second level or deeper has a very small chance (3%) to leave one, and any guardian of the Threefold Deep a slightly better one (6%); a first-level guardian elsewhere never does.
- Release 263 — arriving on a dungeon level never puts you right beside its guardian. Going down a level, coming back after being felled, or re-entering, you're moved along the floor to the nearest spot at least 8 tiles from every living guardian.
- Release 263 — the gap between world beasts counts 8 different sections in a row, not 8 never-visited ones. Going back into one of the last 8 sections you walked through neither adds to the count nor resets it.
- Release 263 — the Mountain Turtle at sea no longer freezes a trail. It leaves one patch of ice where it settles after a combo, and the ice thaws about 8 seconds later whoever is standing on it: if it melts under you, you drop into the deep water.
- Release 263 — hull shields. The Shipwright sells them (40 marks). Deploy one from the Pack's Supplies tab while aboard, and your skiff's hull takes no damage for 3 minutes; a pale ring shows around the skiff while it holds.
- Release 263 — Act at deep water's edge builds a skiff, boards it and pushes off in one press, even with a tree or rock beside you (when you have the wood and the Tidemantle).
- Release 263 — a shovel digs 2 blocks before it snaps (it was 6). The next dig lashes a new one together automatically while you have 1 wood and 1 stone. A shovel you already hold keeps its remaining digs.
- Release 263 — Spells A and B sit beside the compass on every screen: phone open or closed, portrait or landscape. Map, Pack and the preset button stay beneath them.

- Release 261 — world beasts are rarer. Their chances are halved, and after you meet one (or the Questing Beast), no new one appears until you have set foot in 8 sections you had never visited before. Ground you have not yet explored now holds small towns of its own, roughly one every dozen-plus sections: hamlets, villages, walled outposts and market towns, each with a name, a square, two or three huts, a Wayglass to anchor fast travel, residents who talk about the land around them, wardens at the edges and, in most, a trader. - Release 109 — the Arcanum, and both capstones. A mastered constellation branch can now take both of its capstones, each for 2 points: Motion can hold Aerial Step and Slipstream together, Form both Colossus and Cinder Heart, and so on. Spells, disciplines and capstones now live in one place, the Arcanum's Spells & Constellation page (opened from the Pack, which no longer keeps separate spell and discipline lists). - Release 108 — underwater grottoes, the last stage of the water world. Every new dive now leads into one of four grottoes: the Drowned Chapel, the Kelp Cathedral, the Pearl Trench or the Sunken Settlement, each with its own stone, kelp, pearl or ruined-street look. They are longer climbs with currents that push you back down (slower to swim against, never impossible), air pockets within one breath, and grotto beasts: Grotto Eels that charge along a wake line, Lantern Anglers whose glowing lure shows even while they hide, and heavy Abyssal Beasts. An Elder Wyrm coils in the last chamber, guarding the sunken cache: its long body weaves toward you, then it marks a line and lunges. Felling your first Elder Wyrm pays a trophy (40 marks and a weapon sphere). Dives you already made keep their Sunken Reach.
- Release 107 — diving, the fifth stage of the water world. Inspect an island landmark (Tide Shrine, Wrecked Hull or Drowned Beacon) to find the Bathysal Lens, and buy a dive rig from a Shipwright (80 marks and 2 cinder iron, once you own a skiff). With both, the red-and-white dive buoys that float over about one in three stretches of deep water let you dive into the Sunken Reach: a winding seabed cave of kelp under a blue wash of light. Your air shows next to your health and lasts 20 seconds; bubbling air pockets along the way refill it, and if it runs out you start to drown. A sunken cache waits at the far end, and the surface line takes you back up to the buoy, aboard your skiff if you dove from it. If you drown, the line hauls you back to the buoy with fresh air and nothing lost.
- Release 106 — leviathans, the fourth stage of the water world. Out in the deep ocean, roughly one in four big stretches of deep water now holds a single giant. The Greywhale drifts peacefully until you strike it; then it sounds beneath you, its vast shadow spreads, and it breaches, throwing you back. The Tyrant Mosasaur stalks below with only its shadow showing, marks its lunge, tears across the surface, and then lies exposed for a moment. Three krakens: the Reef Kraken marks rings in the water and tentacles burst up through them (each limb can be cut down, never more than three at once); the Abyssal Kraken marks you, then seizes and drags you toward its maw until heavy blows break its grip; the Stormkraken raises a tentacle at a time and clouds the sea with ink. The first time you fell each leviathan you earn a trophy of 40 marks and a weapon sphere. All five have bestiary entries.
- Release 105 — sea creatures, the third stage of the water world. Seas, lakes and swamps you have not yet visited now hold creatures that live only in the water and warn before every strike. Lanceeels hide, show a pale wake line, then charge straight along it (step off the line). Ringcoil Serpents circle you, tightening until they bite; hitting one throws the coil wide. Sinewake Serpents weave toward you in an S with a long trailing body, bite, then back off. Breachfins mark a dark landing shadow and leap out in an arc; they can only be hit in the air. In the fens, Mire Leeches hop through the bog water and Bog Lurkers wait unseen, then bite and drag you a step closer. Hits taken aboard the skiff are shared with its hull. Every creature has a bestiary entry. Fix: resuming a save while aboard the skiff no longer moves you to shore.
- Release 104 — the Skiff, the second stage of the water world. Coasts and lakes you have not yet visited can hold a dock: a Shipwright on the shore beside a short plank pier. Once you hold the Tidemantle she sells a skiff for 120 marks and 2 cinder iron. Board at the berth and sail the sea, deep ocean, lakes, swamp pools and shallows at 1.4x walking speed with no stamina cost; the skiff has momentum, and a dodge is a quick oar burst. Sail into dry land to step ashore; the skiff stays moored where you left it, and you board it again from the shore. The skiff has its own hull (60): hits taken aboard are split with it, and if the hull breaks the skiff sinks, you swim, and the Shipwright's crew returns it to its dock, where she patches it for marks. Blight water and rivers cannot be sailed.
- Release 103 — Waterbodies, the first stage of the water world. Sections you have never visited can now hold true seas that span dozens of sections, with deep open water (swimmable with the Tidemantle, but it tires you twice as fast), scattered islands whose land follows the biome around them, and an island landmark (Tide Shrine, Wrecked Hull or Drowned Beacon) in each stretch of sea. Inland there are lakes, swamps (Sunken Fen: bog ground, slow mire, deep pools) and poison swamps (Blight Mire): wading or swimming in its green murk rots you, lowering your maximum health until you rest at a Wayglass or rest point. Sections you have already explored keep their terrain exactly.
- Release 102 — the Tidemantle, stage one of water traversal. Fell the guardian of a flooded dungeon (one with water in its halls) or a Mire Apostle and you earn the Tidemantle once: from then on rivers, flooded halls and open sea are swimmable at a little over half speed. Swimming stops stamina regeneration and drains it; an exhausted swimmer takes damage until they reach shore. There is no diving: lava, pits, chasms and canyons stay lethal, and without the Tidemantle deep water still drowns.
- Release 101 — falls are real again: walking into a chasm, lava, a river or off a cliff of four or more levels kills (the release-97 edge guard is gone; rims, fog hatching and level marks keep hazards readable). Deep ocean water is now enterable and drowns you; shallows stay walkable. Every landscape section and themed dungeon with one-level steps gets walkable ramps between levels (about one per short run, at least one each). Controls: Jump moves to the centre of the action diamond and the compass takes Jump's old place. Leech heals more (12% weapon, 8% tool, 5% spell; Blood Tide doubles it) and every heal shows a green +N over the Wayfarer.
- Release 100 — the Aperture Constellation and Awakenings complete the itemization rework. Disciplines become five branches (Form, Motion, Veil, Force and the new Rift with Rift Step, a ledge-safe blink), each with its active's ranks, a modifier and a choice of two capstones (Colossus or Cinder Heart; Aerial Step or Slipstream; Sanctuary or Mirror Veil; Amplifier or Resonant Force; Fold or Anchor). Points come from Aperture, one per 6; ranks and modifiers cost 1, capstones 2. Everything learned before this release is kept as bonus points, and a learned Aerial Step becomes the Motion capstone. Shrines gain Attune, the only place to respec (lumen dust). At Sela's forge a max-rank weapon holding a Resonance can be Awakened for 6 lumen dust: awakened weapons release a piercing crescent every third swing; awakened tools fan every shot into three. Forge lab scenario with everything ready.
- Release 99 — Creature Echoes, itemization part two. Every pack creature has an Echo of its signature move (15). Guardians always leave theirs the first time, other pack kills about one in twenty-five. Echoes cost 1-3 of three notches and trigger from what you already do, with no new buttons: dodges (Thunderhorn's Charge tramples, Lynx Pounce empowers the next strike, Pallid Skitter saves stamina, Spinner's Web slows, Eel's Phase extends invulnerability), landing a jump (Sunderjaw's Rupture), kills (Maw's Hunger pulls, Flesh Bloom slows, Seraph's Firefall ignites), being hurt (Choir's Lament strikes back, Mirror Swap trades places), casting (Wraith Lantern beam), firing (Beetle Mortar every third shot), facing foes (Saint's Regard slows them) and standing still (Lantern Stillness mends). New Echoes screen in the Pack; Forge lab Echoes sandbox.
- Release 98 — Glyphs & Resonances, the first part of the itemization rework. Ten glyphs (Ignite, Chain, Pierce, Echo, Leech, Heavy, Swift, Volatile, Tether, Still) set into weapon sockets (1-3 by tier) and two sockets per spell; the same glyph does something different in a weapon, a tool or a spell. Guardians always drop a glyph, other kills about one in eight; salvage yields glyph dust and returns socketed glyphs; 6 dust inscribes any glyph you have found. Every save gets Ignite, Chain and Echo once. New Glyphs screen (Pack and Sela's Forge) and a Glyph Forge lab in the Proving Ground.
- Release 97 — traversal you can trust. Every raised level now looks the same everywhere (overworld, dungeons, Underlands): a lighter wash per level, a coloured trim (bronze 1, teal 2, gold 3, violet 4, white 5) and that many carved notches; ground stays plain, and the area label shows GROUND or LEVEL N. Beside you, cyan chevrons mark a ledge you can jump up to and red crosses one that is too high. Generation now guarantees every floor you can reach leads back out (no more trap holes: 24% of themed dungeons and about 10% of landscape sections had one). Pits, lava and deep water get a crumbling lit rim, the chasm is indigo instead of black, and unexplored fog is a hatched slate; walking or jumping stops at the edge of a pit or a fatal (4+ level) drop, and only a dodge roll carries you over. New Atlas button "Stuck? Get me out": dungeons return you to the level's entrance, the overworld to the nearest ground that leads out (free, 30 s cooldown).
- Release 96 — Underlands: new crossings use generator 5 with six layout grammars (spine, hub, ring, cavern, crypt, descent) and, in about 45% of them, a bespoke underground environment (Sunless Chasm with climbable ledges and a bottomless abyss, Lava Bog with drifting embers, Underground River with bridges and dripping water, Frozen Grotto with falling snow, Bone Crypt, Fungal Hollow with spores).
