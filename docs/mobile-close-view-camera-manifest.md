# Mobile Close View camera pass

## Owner request

Improve follow and navigation through cramped interiors without losing the existing Close View atmosphere. The camera currently feels wonky and too cramped; the player should not have to manage it constantly. Research mobile dungeon crawlers, choose the appropriate behavior, and retain all prior creature, form, input and asset work. No release number is reserved.

The owner subsequently added lock comfort: lower the difficult-to-reach lock button, make acquisition/switching more accurate, keep the camera focused on the selected enemy and have the Wayfarer visibly face it. Elden Ring is a control-behavior reference, not a request to copy an unpublished algorithm or change hit rules.

## Primary-source research

- [Pascal's Wager official controls guidance](https://pw.giant.games/html/news/1343ab4/000-1579-0001f-179609.shtml) documents adjustable camera sensitivity for its mobile controls. This confirms manual camera comfort is a supported setting; it does not expose the game's internal collision/follow algorithm.
- [Torchlight: Infinite official update notes](https://torchlight.xd.com/cn/news/single?id=ejabluQro4IO) describe an enabled-by-default Camera Follow option for smoother movement during non-Blink mobility skills. This supports deliberate movement-follow behavior rather than treating the camera as a fixed spectator. Its implementation is not published.
- [Unity's Third Person Follow documentation](https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.cinemachine@3.1/manual/CinemachineThirdPersonFollow.html) describes an offset follow rig, camera clearance radius, obstacle correction and separate damping into/from collisions. These are implementation principles for our custom renderer, not a proposal to add Unity or Cinemachine dependencies.
- [Elden Ring's official starter guide](https://en.bandainamcoent.eu/elden-ring/news/elden-ring-starter-guide-tips-know-playing-the-game) describes locking a nearby enemy with a button, switching targets with the right stick and keeping the target in front. The adopted mobile analogue is a reachable lock button plus deliberate left/right camera gestures. Visible-first selection, a bounded forward fallback, raw finger-distance thresholds and the exact placement are our design inferences; the guide does not publish proprietary targeting algorithms.

## Proposed behavior to validate

Use an assisted third-person chase style suited to the current immersive interiors: responsive positional follow, deliberate recentering after movement, manual right-drag override, and source lock-on behavior. In confined geometry, prioritize a readable character and nearby danger with centered shoulder framing, a bounded elevated view and safe distance changes. Resolve collisions promptly, recover smoothly, and avoid repeated zoom/side/angle oscillation. Do not change dungeon geometry or regenerate old rooms to make the camera fit.

These are design inferences from the sources and the current defects, not claims that one published game exposes a universally best algorithm. Audit current camera code and reproduce defects before selecting exact parameters.

## Source audit and reproduced defects

The existing rig already has positional springs with bounded lag, a travel-heading yaw spring, a 1.2-second manual-look grace period and 1.2-second recovery ramp, a 100-degree follow cone that excludes backpedaling, target lock, a latched movement basis, collision pull-in/let-out rates, an elevated compressed view and a body-framing constraint. Preset distances/FOV, sensitivity and unlocked follow rates are retained in this pass. A sustained 90-degree movement turn is followed to within 15 degrees after 5.28 seconds without rotating the held stick's world vector; reduced motion follows more slowly and the saved opt-out prevents it entirely.

Reproductions on the existing source before this pass:

- In the production-backed `close-view` / `cellar` lab, place the player at tile `(11, 5)`, face the camera south (yaw π/2), disable auto-follow, and render 450 stationary 16 ms frames at 390×844. During frames 351–450 the boom repeatedly ranged from **1.386 to 2.642 tiles**. The previous wide clearance check measured the already-shortened eye and cleared its limit on the next safe frame; shoulder recovery then reintroduced the corner obstruction. A sweep of 35 existing open tiles × eight headings reproduced the same worst case without changing the room.
- A pending recenter from north toward east advanced from −π/2 to zero over 20 paused renders. Camera springs and absolute quick-turn/manual-grace clocks were not suspended. The ordinary main loop also skips rendering during pause, so render-only pause handling is insufficient.
- The pointer handlers retained a captured camera thumb but only refreshed manual suppression on movement or queued pan. Holding that thumb still beyond the grace period allowed travel-follow to take over.

## Implemented behavior

- Compute the centered boom's requested clearance before applying its spring. Test the entire player-to-shoulder line, not only the shoulder endpoint. Obstructed shoulder clearance contracts immediately; expansion waits for 0.25 seconds of clear space and then uses the existing recovery spring. No side switching or camera yaw avoidance is introduced.
- Center shoulder and transverse positional lag when boom clearance falls below 55% of the saved preset distance; restore them continuously up to 90%. This prevents a small boom from turning ordinary sideways follow lag into an off-screen hood in portrait. Longitudinal follow, the bounded crane, arch/ceiling/terrain checks and final hard collision checks remain active.
- Freeze camera motion and timers during pause. `Game.setPaused` accumulates elapsed pause duration in a runtime-only presentation counter, consumed once by the camera. This covers scenes, Glimpses and background suspension with no camera/input callbacks at all. Direct paused-frame/input sampling remains a fallback for callers that set the flag directly. Resuming does not skip through a recenter/quick-turn or consume the manual grace period.
- A captured camera thumb keeps manual suppression active even while stationary; release returns to the existing delayed assisted chase. Movement-pointer ownership, target-relative lock, explicit recenter, quick-turn, reduced motion, sensitivity, preset settings and default eligibility are preserved.

All added camera state is preallocated runtime state. Clearance adds one bounded line probe; there are no new per-frame objects, route searches, world edits, saved fields or dependencies. No actor geometry, asset eligibility, hit rules, generator, package, workflow, script or release-number changes are included.

## Lock comfort audit and implementation

The original button remained above the entire crescent even when default drag mode hid that rail. It accepted only 10 pixels of release drift. Target acquisition did not prioritize actual viewport visibility. Switching multiplied swipe distance by camera sensitivity and acceleration, allowing repeated switches during one gesture. Retained locks did not recheck hidden/untargetable eligibility; camera follow continued during LOS grace and stopped entirely below 0.75 tile. The native Wayfarer's idle/strafe facing followed travel even though gameplay aim followed the selected foe.

- Default drag mode places a **52-pixel lock target** in the action cross's upper-right empty cell beside Tool. Actual visible button rectangles are checked, with at most 32 upward 8-pixel placement steps if that cell is occupied. The tested portrait layout places it approximately 110 pixels lower than the former off-rail position. Crescent mode retains an off-rail position to keep camera gestures separate. The lock accepts 18 pixels of release drift; camera-drag thresholds are unchanged.
- Acquisition prioritizes projected visible enemies in front of the camera, then the existing angle/distance score within that group. With none visible, a target within **six tiles and 100 degrees** of forward may be acquired, still requiring player LOS and normal eligibility. Overall range stays 11 tiles. There is no blind rear or through-wall acquisition. Explicit death-rehop behavior retains its previous local search.
- A deliberate horizontal drag switches once per captured gesture after **26 raw pixels**. Both drag and crescent modes use physical gesture travel, independent of sensitivity/acceleration. Lift and gesture again to switch again. Visible neighbors take priority; nearby offscreen switching remains bounded.
- Newly hidden, untargetable, veiled or otherwise ineligible selections release promptly. The existing **1.2-second LOS grace** retains the id through a brief obstruction, while camera yaw, visual look and steering basis stop tracking the hidden motion; sustained obstruction releases. Existing game attack/aim rules are untouched.
- Near-target follow slows to 35% of the existing spring rate rather than stopping. Locked yaw has a **120-degree/second cap**, scaled down for reduced motion. This keeps a foe at melee distance under camera follow without an unlimited whip.
- A preallocated presentation-only idle lock vector directs native and fallback Wayfarer rendering toward the visible selected enemy. The one native-admission fallback hunk is explicitly coordinated with the parent hero integration. Accepted attack records, charge direction, casting, stun, `player.facing`, saved aim and attack snapshots are not rewritten. Existing action aim takes precedence.

Lock look adds bounded LOS checks; screen projection/scoring occurs only on acquisition/switch/rehop, and button rectangle allocation occurs during UI layout rather than the render hot path. No save-schema or combat-rule change is introduced.

## Independent review follow-up

Independent review reproduced two lifecycle/control defects in the initial pass. A quick-turn paused for five seconds through `Game.setPaused`, with no render or steering callbacks during the pause, jumped from yaw 0.600697 to π and completed on resume. Separately, a stationary held camera thumb reset the lock spring's velocity each frame: after 2.88 seconds a near target at bearing 0.643501 was followed only to 0.035174 radians instead of 0.605326.

Both are corrected. The narrowly scoped `src/game.ts` change records cumulative runtime presentation pause duration after the existing `shiftClocks` call, preserving its exact delta and every existing action/save clock. Camera consumption supersedes sampled pause bookkeeping to prevent a double shift. The new counter never enters exported saves. Held-pointer velocity suppression now applies only to unlocked auto-follow; selected-target follow retains its spring velocity. Focused regressions cover both exact reproductions, action-clock/snapshot preservation, and manual grace after a callback-free pause.

CI on candidate `4352b0a9` exposed two further integration issues. The cohesion test exercised crescent gesture ownership but mounted the default drag layout; it now explicitly selects crescent mode while retaining its off-rail, tap, rejected-drag and pointer-ownership assertions. Drag-mode reach/clearance remains covered separately. The native Pentagon test found a real first-paused-render defect: a newly admitted creature's opacity fade initialized at zero, and the frozen camera delta prevented it from ever becoming visible. Diagnostic uploads contained two models with alpha values only 1 and 0, confirming admission rather than a camera-framing failure. New fade history created while paused now initializes from computed lighting/fog/cut visibility; existing history stays frozen and active-play fade-in is unchanged. The original positive-alpha ≤0.45 and residual-FX assertions remain, with stronger equality checking both paused geometry and opacity. No hit rule, alpha threshold or test skip changed.
